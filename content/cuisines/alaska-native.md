Alaska is home to many Native peoples, each with its own language and way of eating. The Alaska Native Heritage Center groups them into five regions: Iñupiaq and St. Lawrence Island Yupik in the north, Yup'ik and Cup'ik in the southwest, Unangax̂ and Alutiiq in the Aleutians and Southcentral, Athabascans in the Interior, and Eyak, Tlingit, Haida and Tsimshian in the Southeast.

Food comes from the land and water close to home: [[Salmon]] and other fish, [[Seal oil]] and sea mammals on the coast, [[Caribou]] and [[Moose]] inland, and berries and greens picked in summer. Hunting, fishing, gathering and sharing with family and Elders are still at the center of how people eat today.
