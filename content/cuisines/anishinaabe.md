Anishinaabe oral tradition tells of a journey west to the place where food grows on water. That food is [[Wild rice|manoomin]], wild rice, and it still feeds Ojibwe communities in Minnesota, Wisconsin and Michigan in body and spirit. At harvest, ricers pole or paddle small boats through the beds and knock the ripe grain in with cedar sticks, going back again and again as it ripens.

Late winter is sugarbush time, when families return to the same maple stands, some kept for generations, to boil sap into [[Maple syrup|syrup]] and sugar. Maple sugar seasons food, and sugar cakes molded into people, animals and moons are given as gifts.
