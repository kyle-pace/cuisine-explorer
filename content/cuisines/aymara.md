Aymara families farm the Bolivian highlands, 3,000 to 4,000 meters up, where the sun is strong and the nights freeze. After the [[Potato|potato]] harvest, they sort the crop three ways: to eat soon, to plant next year, and to turn into [[Chuño]]. On frosty nights in June and July those potatoes are left out to freeze, then trodden to squeeze out the water, peeled and dried hard in the sun, and they can keep for decades. Tunta starts the same way but is soaked in a running river, which turns it white.

On Lake Titicaca, Aymara fishers have eaten karachi, a tiny native fish, for thousands of years, fried with salt or added to soups, and trade it for [[Quinoa]] and potatoes on market days. Overfishing and trout, brought into the lake in the 1930s, now threaten it.
