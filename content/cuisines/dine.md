Diné people live across the Navajo Nation in the Four Corners, and corn is at the heart of the table. [[Blue corn]] is cooked into mush, dumplings and bread, often with juniper ash stirred in, and fresh corn is ground, wrapped in husks and baked in a pit as [[Kneel down bread]]. Gardens grow corn, beans, squash and melons, and families travel to gather wild sumac berries, chokecherries and banana yucca.

Sheep have been part of Diné life since the 1600s, so much that a well known Diné saying calls sheep life itself. [[Lamb and mutton|Mutton]] stew with potatoes, carrots and sometimes [[Hominy]] is a classic, served with [[Frybread]] or tortillas. Flour, sugar and coffee came after 1868 and are still in Diné kitchens, while summits and cooking classes pass the older recipes to younger cooks.
