For the Guaraní, [[Maize|corn]], avaxi, is sacred. Mbyá families in Brazil's Atlantic Forest keep old varieties like avaxi etei, and some foods are meant for certain times: one corn is recommended for new mothers, eaten with purple [[Sweet potato|sweet potato]]. Jera Guarani and community elders gathered corn recipes in a book called Ara Pyau, new time.

Mbyá women pound corn into dough with a pestle for a corn cake baked in a clay pot over coals in a hole in the ground, covered with banana leaves, and they make [[Chipá|chipa]] too. Long before wheat arrived, the Guaraní relied on [[Cassava|manioc]] as much as on corn, and in Paraguay chipa of manioc flour is still baked in a tatakua, a brick and mud oven whose Guaraní name means fire hole.
