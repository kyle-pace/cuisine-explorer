For the Haudenosaunee, among them the Seneca, Mohawk, Oneida, Onondaga and Tuscarora, white corn has been prized for about a thousand years. It grows with beans and squash as the [[Three Sisters]], and women have long led nearly every step of growing and preparing it.

The corn is planted, picked and husked by hand, braided and hung to dry, then hulled, traditionally with hardwood ash, into [[Hominy|hulled corn]] for soups and stews. Roasted and stone ground, it becomes a nutty flour for mush, bread and baking. At Ganondagan in Victor, New York, the Iroquois White Corn Project grows it and encourages Haudenosaunee families to eat it every day, not only on special occasions.
