Kichwa families in Ecuador's Amazon grow food in the chakra, a forest garden that FAO lists as a Globally Important Agricultural Heritage System. In Alto Tena, a chakra holds lumu, [[Cassava|yuca]], kakau, [[Cacao|cacao]], and its wild cousins, chunta palm fruit, kumal, [[Sweet potato|sweet potato]], uchu muyu chiles, manduru, [[Achiote|achiote]], and dozens of forest fruits and nuts.

Visitors are welcomed with [[Chicha]] of yuca, which women mostly make. The boiled roots are mashed in a wooden trough, chewed and returned so saliva breaks down the starch, then left to ferment for a couple of days or longer, thinned with water and served in a clay bowl called a mucawa. [[Guayusa]], a native leaf, is traditionally brewed and sipped in the morning, and Kichwa entrepreneur Esthela Noteno now bottles it from her grandmother's recipe.
