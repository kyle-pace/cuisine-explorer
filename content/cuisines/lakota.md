Lakota life on the Northern Plains centers on [[Bison]], tatanka, a sacred animal that gives food, clothing, shelter and tools. Meat is dried in thin strips, and dried meat mixed with fat and chokecherries becomes wasna, the Lakota form of [[Pemmican]], made to last through hard winters. Corn came by trade with the Mandan villages.

Wild berries, traditionally chokecherries, are cooked into [[Wojapi]], eaten with [[Frybread]] or alongside meat. Today bison are returning to tribal lands, and Oglala Lakota chef Sean Sherman cooks with Indigenous foods only, leaving out wheat flour, dairy and cane sugar.
