Mapuche cooking follows the seasons, built on grains and legumes gathered through the year. In the Araucanía, piñones, the big seeds of the araucaria tree, are cooked, sautéed with garlic, oregano and salt, and finished with [[Merkén]], smoked chile ground with spices. Spring brings digüeñes, chewy mushrooms that grow on oak trees, for salads, stews and empanadas, and maqui berries go into jams and drinks.

Tortillas are baked under hot ashes, mültrün is a long wheat bread eaten with honey or jam, and muday, a lightly fermented grain drink, is part of the nguillatún ceremony. In Curarrehue, near Pucón, Ana Epulef, who learned from her mother and grandmother, has run the restaurant and school Mapu Iyagl since 2003, and in Santiago José Luis Calfucura opened a Mapuche restaurant in 2015.
