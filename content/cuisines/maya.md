Maya families in the Yucatán Peninsula and the highlands of Guatemala still eat from the milpa, the field where [[Maize|corn]], [[Beans|beans]] and [[Squash (plant)|squash]] grow together, along with root crops like [[Cassava]] and [[Sweet potato]] and fruit trees in the home garden. Corn is sacred in Maya tradition and shows up at almost every meal, as tortillas or as [[Tamale|tamales]] wrapped in leaves, and [[Achiote]] turns many dishes red.

In Yucatán, pib means cooking underground. During Hanal Pixán, families in towns like Kinchil fill [[Pib|pibes]] of corn dough, wrap them in banana leaves and bury them in an earth oven of fire-heated stones, and the first one out goes on the altar for relatives who have died. [[Cochinita pibil]], pork marinated in achiote and sour orange, is traditionally cooked the same way. In Alta Verapaz, Guatemala, Q'eqchi' cooks make [[Kak'ik]], a spicy red turkey soup the country named part of its heritage in 2007, and highland kitchens simmer pepián with toasted pumpkin and sesame, and green jocón.
