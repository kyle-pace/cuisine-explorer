The Mixteca has thin soil and few ingredients, so Mixtec cooks get a lot out of [[Maize|corn]], [[Beans|beans]] and [[Chili pepper|chile]]. From Huajuapan de León come chileajo, pork in chile sauce, mole de guajes, made with the pods of the guaje tree, tetelas, large corn tortillas filled with beans, and chilate, a spicy drink of toasted corn, [[Cacao|cacao]] and chile. Much of it depends on the chile costeño.

Goats came with the Spanish, and Mixtec families have raised them for centuries. Each October in the Tehuacán valley of southern Puebla, the matanza turns the year's goats into mole de caderas, a stew of the hindquarters with chile costeño, avocado leaves and guajes. Cooks like Olga Cabrera, from Huajuapan, now take Mixtec dishes to festivals as far away as New York.
