Nahua communities speak Nahuatl, and many still farm the mīllah, a cornfield where much more than corn grows. In Chicontepec, in the Huasteca of Veracruz, fresh tlaxcalli, tortillas, are made at midday, and etl, [[Beans|beans]], go into refried beans, soups and bean [[Tamale|tamales]] called piquiz, nearly always with [[Chili pepper|chile]].

In the mountains of Zongolica, Veracruz, the Siwalcentli Collective of Nahua women shares their recipes: tesmole, a stew served with little balls of [[Masa|masa]], fresh corn tamales, tortillas cooked on a clay comal, wild greens like mixquilitl, and popotocas, insects eaten in soup or fried with salsa. Epazote, corn, squash and tiny citlaltomatl tomatoes all grow close to home.
