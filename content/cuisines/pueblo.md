Pueblo people in New Mexico and Arizona still grow corn, beans and squash, the [[Three Sisters]], for everyday meals and for ceremony, and corn holds a special place in spiritual life. Ears are roasted after the fall harvest, white corn is roasted and dried into chicos for stews, and dried, strung chile seasons much of the food.

Bread comes out of hornos, outdoor adobe ovens, and red chile with pork is a familiar dish. At Ohkay Owingeh, Norma and Hutch Naranjo teach these foods at The Feasting Place, and in Albuquerque the Indian Pueblo Kitchen cooks with [[Blue corn]], red and green chile and local produce. At [[Hopi foodways|Hopi]], cooks make paper-thin [[Piki]].
