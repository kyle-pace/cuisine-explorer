When UNESCO recognized traditional Mexican cuisine, it named Michoacán as the model: a way of eating built on [[Maize|corn]], [[Beans|beans]] and [[Chili pepper|chile]], grown in the milpa, made into dough by [[Nixtamalization]] and ground on stone, and kept alive by collectives of women cooks. Two cocineras from the region, Antonina González Leandro of Tarerio and Juanita Bravo Lázaro of Angahuan, traveled to Nairobi to receive it.

Around Lake Pátzcuaro and up on the Meseta Purépecha, cocineras tradicionales keep the dishes going. Corundas, a kind of [[Tamale|tamal]], come plain or with ash, chard or cheese; uchepos of fresh corn come with cream and salsa; churipo is served with plain corundas; atápakua is made with nopal or charales, small lake fish; and there are atoles of every kind. Each year they cook, teach and compete at the Encuentro de Cocina Tradicional, Michoacán's largest food festival.
