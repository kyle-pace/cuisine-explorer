For Quechua farmers in the Andes, the native [[Potato|potato]] is the most basic food. In the Potato Park near Pisaq, Cusco, six Quechua communities organized as ayllus look after about 1,400 kinds of potato and other tubers, along with [[Quinoa]], kiwicha ([[Amaranth]]), tarwi, oca, olluco, corn and beans, and they raise [[Llama|llamas]], alpacas and guinea pigs. Families high up trade potatoes for quinoa and corn grown farther down.

In the coldest months, potatoes are left out to freeze at night and dry in the sun until they're black and hard. That's [[Chuño]], and it keeps for a decade or more. Moraya is freeze-dried in running water until it turns white. Fresh potatoes are boiled whole in their skins or baked in a watya, a small oven built of earth and heated with wood. At planting, [[Coca leaf|coca leaves]] and llama fat go into the first hole as an offering to Pachamama.
