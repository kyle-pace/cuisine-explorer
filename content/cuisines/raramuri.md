[[Maize|Corn]] is the most important crop for the Rarámuri of the Sierra Tarahumara, grown in family plots and milpas. Popped and ground, it becomes [[Pinole]] and esquiate. Many children now eat industrial meals at boarding schools, so the Rarámuri group NATIKA asked researchers from UNAM to film families making these foods, to help bring them back to the table.

Families live far apart in the mountains, and [[Tesgüino]], a thick corn beer the Rarámuri call sowiki, brings them together. Women sprout the corn, grind it, boil it for hours and ferment it in clay pots, and when the pots are opened it's first offered to Onorúame, the Father. It's shared at gatherings for healing, births and deaths, planting and harvest, ball races and house building, after the men have worked the host's fields together.
