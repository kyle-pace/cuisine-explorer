Spanish officials declared the Taíno extinct by the 1550s, but their culture survived, and today people in Puerto Rico, Cuba, the Dominican Republic and Jamaica who identify as Taíno say they are still here. Taíno words for foods and farming live on in Caribbean speech, and yuca, [[Cassava]], their staple, is still eaten, not least as a survival food when hurricanes hit.

[[Casabe]], a round bread of grated, dried cassava baked on a griddle, is still a daily food in much of the region, and in 2024 UNESCO recognized cassava bread from Cuba, the Dominican Republic, Haiti, Honduras and Venezuela, with its Indigenous and African roots. In Puerto Rico, Taíno tubers and [[Achiote]] season pasteles, and the conuco, a small farm plot, helped rural families feed themselves into the 1900s. Some young Puerto Rican farmers and chefs see these practices as a way back to Taíno foods.
