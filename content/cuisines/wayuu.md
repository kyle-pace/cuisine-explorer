The Wayuu, the largest Indigenous people in Colombia, cook from both the sea and the dry land of La Guajira: fish and shellfish, [[Maize|corn]], beans, squash, [[Cassava|yuca]], and [[Goat|goat]] above all. Friche, goat meat and offal cooked in its own juices and then fried in its fat, is made for special occasions and to honor guests, with grilled arepa, bollo or boiled yuca. The fogón, the kitchen fire, is meant to stay lit and is where the family gathers.

Corn drinks feed the family: ookostuushi, a corn mazamorra for children, and uujolu, a [[Chicha|chicha]] without alcohol, for hot days and for marriage proposals. In Manaure, Wayuu families grow kapeshuna, a small bean sown before the rains, and women save the seed in gourds until the next season. It goes into poy, a thick soup with yellow corn and lamb fat.
