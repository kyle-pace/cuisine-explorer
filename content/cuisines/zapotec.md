In Oaxaca's Central Valleys, Zapotec women make and sell [[Tejate]], a cool drink of [[Nixtamalization|nixtamalized]] corn, toasted [[Cacao]], mamey seeds and rosita de cacao, a fragrant flower. Everything is ground into a dough and slowly thinned with water until pale curds float on top, and it's served in jícaras, painted gourd bowls. San Andrés Huayápam, which has grown rosita de cacao since the 1500s, holds a tejate fair with about 150 vendors.

Abigail Mendoza Ruiz, a Zapotec cook, has run her restaurant Tlamanalli since 1990. She cooks [[Mole|moles]], among them a pre-Hispanic mole with yerba santa, pipián of pumpkin seeds, and chicken broth with fresh corn and squash blossoms, and she gathered her recipes in a book called Dishdaa'w.
