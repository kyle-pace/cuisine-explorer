A round bread of [[Cassava]], usually bitter cassava, which helps it keep. The root is peeled, grated and dried, the flour is sifted, and it's cooked on a clay or iron griddle, often over a wood fire. It's a daily staple in much of the Caribbean and Central America, and in 2024 UNESCO recognized the cassava bread of Cuba, the Dominican Republic, Haiti, Honduras and Venezuela, with its Indigenous and African roots. [[Garifuna foodways|Garifuna]] women call it ereba and spend days making it to share.
