Frybread comes out of hunger. When the U.S. government forced the [[Diné foodways|Diné]] on the Long Walk to land that couldn't support their crops, and pushed other nations off their homelands, it handed out rations of white flour, lard, sugar and salt. Fried dough from those rations kept families alive.

Today it's a powwow staple and the base of Indian tacos, and it holds both pride and pain: a sign of survival, and a reminder of the foods that were lost. It's also high in fat and calories. Some critics tie it to diabetes on reservations, while others say the deeper problem is access to healthy food, and many communities are bringing traditional foods back alongside it.
